# Make every page say "hire me for content engineering", 3 Nov 2026

**Goal (Peace, 3 Nov):** anyone who lands on any page must see, in their own vocabulary, that she is someone to hire for content engineering: SEO and AEO writing, content strategy, and AI applied to systems and workflows that make a content team's life easier. Not "AI writes the content".

**Method:** three reader personas reviewed the rendered pages in parallel and independently, the way Nate Herk's "roast room" council works (one question each, evidence only, no softening): a B2B SaaS CMO with a $2k to $3k monthly writer budget; the founder of a 12-person SEO agency; a Series B Head of Content and SEO hiring a senior content engineer, who also audited the site as an SEO. I verified every technical claim below against the local build. Items marked **(Peace)** need facts or permissions only she has.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

All three would book the call, but narrowly, and none because of the first screen. The CMO leaned no after the homepage alone. What decided it for each: the ManyRequests page-one screenshot with its honest caveat, the Anthropic policy bug in AI Docs Watch ("152 changed passages, and the writer only saw the first 24"), the "6 of 10 citations" parser catch, the public prices with no lock-in, the handover terms in the services FAQ. What held every one of them back: the first screen talks about Peace and her philosophy, the proof is anonymous, there is no business outcome anywhere, a hobby app (Aloud) holds a third of the homepage, and nothing on the site sells the strategy and AEO work the contact form asks about.

## 1. First screen and homepage

1. **Headline in the buyer's words, proof under it.** Today: "Good work starts with good questions." and "Is this true? / What's worth making? / What does done mean?". A CMO has to read a paragraph under each to learn what the section is. Replace with a line that names what, for whom, and one proof: the SEO lead's version was "SEO and AEO articles for B2B SaaS. AI tools for your content team." The three "question" sections can stay as the structure, but each needs a plain subtitle.
2. **Aloud out of the third pillar.** All three readers called it a hobby app on the page where it is the proof of "done". Put the agency reading app, Quality Rater or Fanout there. Aloud stays as a case study, grouped under "built for myself".
3. **Drop the Lagos and "thanks for scrolling" lines from the hero and footer, or move them to About.** The CMO read them as the site being about the writer. One mention on About is enough.
4. **Translate the jargon on first use:** BOFU, AEO, MCP, fanout, ICP. Two sentences on what the AEO method actually changes on a page and how being cited is measured.

## 2. Proof and outcomes **(Peace)**

5. **A results table for ManyRequests**, not "page one": queries, positions, dates, Search Console clicks and impressions if she can share them, a before and after, and the business-potential-score formula with one topic it rejected. The homepage's single dated screenshot is the only SEO outcome on the site.
6. **One AEO outcome produced with her own Fanout tool:** a prompt set, citation rate before and after, for a client or for her own AEO article. It is the missing link between the tools and the writing; today she built the thing that measures citations and shows no reading from it.
7. **Name the clients on the case studies where allowed** (the agency, the reading app's owner), or say "NDA, agency of N people, SEO and content". The agency founder: "I can't click, test or call anyone". About mentions Steve Toth and Notebook Agency once, inside a paragraph.
8. **A number of use on each team tool:** strategists using it, runs captured a week, weeks in production, hours it replaced. Inputs ("1.23 million words into 683 notes") are not outcomes.
9. **One testimonial about a tool.** Every quote today is about writing.

## 3. Services

10. **Sell the strategy work the form already asks about:** an SEO and AEO offer with a starting scope and price (audit, topic roadmap, AI-search monitoring). Today the page sells $400 to $600 articles and asks the buyer for "a brief or topic with a target keyword", which reads as order-taking.
11. **A range and a calendar for builds** **(Peace)**: "a tool like Fanout: $X to $Y, N weeks, hosting about $Z a month". "No price list for this one" and "ongoing support is a separate arrangement" were the two lines the Series B reader said would make procurement pause. Say who reviews the code and what happens after month one.
12. **A "for agencies" block:** volume (10 to 20 pieces a month), white-label, turnaround, time-zone overlap, what AI does and does not do in her process. HigherVisibility and Spicy Margarita prove she has done agency work; the page never says so.
13. **Capacity and availability:** "I cap at 4 active clients" needs "and I have N open now" or a next-opening date.
14. **Link out from Services** to the AEO article, the ManyRequests case, the relevant tools and the testimonials. It links to none of them today.

## 4. Case studies

15. **Two groups, not one dated list:** "Built for content and SEO teams" (Fanout, AI Docs Watch, Quality Rater, OKF, the reading app) first, ordered by relevance; "Built for myself" (Aloud, Reach, ContentDB) smaller and after. "Things I've built, and how they came together" is diary framing; the intro about blurring is about her process.
16. **The status line answers the wrong question.** "Who can use it: my own app, invite-only" and "personal tool, two Macs" tell a buyer it is a hobby. Rename it "Who uses it" and write it as "N strategists at an SEO agency since 13 Oct 2026" or "Live, public: docswatch.stevetoth.ai". **(Peace)** for the numbers.
17. **Quality Rater measures or steps back.** "first version", "not yet measured", "when the feed is less noisy" appear three times. Either an agreement rate on N pages, or move it below the fold until it has one.
18. **Each case study links to the matching service and article.** Today they link only to two sibling studies and a call.
19. **Rewrite rule (Peace, 3 Nov):** before any case-study text changes, read the project's own files and the build transcripts. Expertise and why it mattered, not technical detail; the informal voice stays. Her Notion edits for seven pages are still being checked and then get folded in.

## 5. About and testimonials

20. **Open with the reader's problem, then Peace.** The page is sixty percent process ("I check, I test, I keep"). One paragraph of each.
21. **The pass-along bio** uses "He" while every other line on the site is "I". Lily Ugbaja's quote also says "He". Both readers flagged it as unproofed; it is the paragraph a buyer forwards. Confirm the wording is what she wants, then make it consistent.
22. **Exceed SEO** (Lateef Maleek's quote) is not in the client list on Services. Add it or say what the work was.
23. **The Garde story and "vibe-coding"** appear on About, Writing & work and the blog. Honest, but three readers took it as "demo that breaks". Keep it once, on the blog, and answer the question it raises: how shipped tools are kept alive (monitoring, tests, who fixes what).

## 6. Contact form

24. **Project types must match Services.** TOFU, MOFU and eBook are offered on the form and excluded on Services. Add "Build a tool or automate a workflow" and "SEO and AEO strategy".
25. **"Where are you in the process?"** is required and every option is about a content strategy. A tool buyer has no honest answer. Add a tool path: the task the team repeats, who would use it, current tools, timeline.
26. **Budget bands** stop at "$5,000+" and do not match per-article prices. Ask per engagement type.
27. "No pressure, it's a chat" is too casual for the buyer we want. "30 minutes, you describe the work, I say whether I'm the right person" is the site's own promise.

## 7. UI, screenshots and colours

28. **Screenshot standard (now partly in code):** a tall phone screenshot gets a 4:5 frame cropped from the top centre; a wide desktop screenshot keeps 16:10. Done today for covers and index thumbnails, after the Aloud cover was a 16:10 crop of a phone's top-left corner.
29. **One capture rule for every app:** same browser width (1440 for desktop, a real phone for mobile), light theme, no dev badges, nothing cut mid-element, identifying client text masked, and a caption that says what to look at. Today the captures mix sizes, themes and crops.
30. **Don't recolour the apps; mat them.** Aloud is orange on cream, Docs Watch is white, the site is navy and paper. The fix is a consistent neutral frame (thin border, paper background, same radius) so the apps keep their identity and the page stays one system. That is the current `.cs-cover-shot` style; apply it to every inline figure too.
31. **Covers show the product, not a logo card.** The Aloud cover now shows the player; the index should lead each card with the most "in use" screen.
32. **Dark mode check on every new screenshot** (the October QA script does this; keep running it).

## 8. Site architecture (SEO)

The sitemap has 52 URLs: 8 top-level pages, 10 case studies, the legacy /projects tree (6 children) and /career-pathway, 9 articles at the root, 15 tag pages, plus three client sample pages that are linked but not in the sitemap.

What is wrong today:
- **"Writing & work" (/portfolio) and "Projects" (/case-studies) are nav siblings with the same nine tools and the same blurbs word for word.** The case-study breadcrumb "Writing & work / Projects" says one is inside the other. Three names for one idea: Projects, Earlier projects (/projects), Writing & work.
- **/projects/contentdb and /case-studies/contentdb both exist** and compete.
- **No page title or H1 targets a buyer query** except the AEO article. Home title is 93 characters and truncates; Services title is "Services and prices"; About is "About".
- **Doubled suffix** "| Peace Akinwale | Peace Akinwale" on /projects, /case-studies/manyrequests, /career-pathway (verified).
- **Schema is only Person and WebSite** on every page; no BreadcrumbList, Article, Service, FAQPage (the services FAQ is real) or SoftwareApplication.
- **Internal links are one-way:** Services to nothing specific; case studies to siblings only; the AEO article links to /services and /contact with `http://` absolute URLs (verified, from WordPress).
- **Sitemap hygiene:** every static page has the same lastmod (build time); thin tag pages are included; /career-pathway and the legacy tree are included; the three client pages are not.

Proposed structure (keeps every URL that matters, 301s the rest):
- Nav: **Services, Case studies, Writing, Blog, About, Contact.** One "Book a call" button.
- /services, with /services/seo-and-aeo-articles, /services/aeo-strategy, /services/content-tools when the offers exist.
- /case-studies stays, labelled "Case studies", grouped as in item 15; breadcrumb "Case studies / Name" with BreadcrumbList schema.
- /portfolio stays, labelled "Writing": articles by client only, no tool blurbs. The client-by-client list, which is what a hiring CMO wants, is the least visible thing on the site today.
- Blog posts: decide whether to move root slugs under /blog/ with 301s (cleaner) or leave them (no link equity lost). My recommendation: leave the URLs, fix the titles, retire the 2024 filler.
- 301 /projects and its children to /case-studies (or the matching study); 301 /services-v2 to /services; noindex /career-pathway and the tag pages; testimonials fold into About.
- Titles: "B2B SaaS Content Writer and AEO Content Engineer | Peace Akinwale" style on the money pages; fix the doubled suffix; real lastmod from git dates.

## 9. The council, as a skill ("rostrum")

Nate Herk's roast room: a chair runs persona sub-agents, each with a researched "brain" (a hot sheet of rules, frameworks, positions, sources, stories, voice) and one question it owns, through three rounds (positions, debate, vote), and saves a verdict. The lesson he draws: outsource the thinking, not the understanding.

Today's three reviews were a manual first run of that for this site. The skill version, `/hiring-council`, would sit beside the existing `/llm-council` (which has generic advisors, not buyers) and give each seat a brain built from Peace's own sources: editor comments and client feedback (Jabra, Marker.io, ManyRequests, Exceed SEO), Granola call notes, Steve's notes on what agencies buy, her AEO research, and her copy rules. Seats: the CMO, the agency founder, the Head of Content and SEO, a design critic holding the taste signals and the no-filler standard, and a copy editor holding her rules. It runs on any page or draft, writes the verdict to `docs/qa/council/<date>-<page>.md`, and is the gate before "publish". Proposed as a separate task; it does not block the fixes above.
