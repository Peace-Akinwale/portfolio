# Testimonials from the Notebook team: what to use, 3 Nov 2026

From the sixteen quotes Peace pulled from Slack and Wispr. Nothing goes on the site until the person says yes, and every quote stays word for word (a cleaned-up spoken quote is used only in the version the speaker approves).

**One conflict to settle first.** Peace's rule is no client names on case studies. A testimonial with "Steve Toth, founder" on it names the client. Two ways through: put named quotes on About and the testimonials page only, and use a role ("the founder of the SEO agency that uses it") when a quote sits on a case study. Each ask below offers the person that choice.

## Ask for a new quote instead

- **Simon Mcmahon** (Senior Backend AEO/SEO Strategist). His day-one line, "Got it all working and am running prompts", is thin. He has used Fanout for about seven weeks now; one sentence on what it changed in his work would be the strategist's view the site lacks.
- **David Lindahl** (Crecera Digital, outside the agency). The only person outside the team who signed in to the reading app after launch. A real user quote from an outside agency is worth more than his launch-day "Bad Ass!".

## Leave these out

- **Steve's "game changer in terms of time savings" (#2).** The sentence opens with "Please don't share publicly". Cutting that clause to use the rest would publish what he asked students not to share.
- **Thank-yous and one-feature remarks** (#4, #9, #11, #13, #14, #15). Genuine, but they read as thanks, not as evidence.
- **Simon on support work (#6) and Gregory reporting what Steve thinks (#8, #10).** About her help or second-hand, not about a tool she built.
- **Nathan Seya (#16).** Not tied to a deliverable, and he has left the agency.
