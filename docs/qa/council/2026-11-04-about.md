## The verdict in one paragraph

Three of five seats would email first, not book. The page says Peace owns the check, but it shows no proof that holds today. No tool has a user count, and the one writing result has lapsed: "one of them sat on page one of Google for “design annotation tools” in April 2026." is true for April and off page one on 3 Nov. The copy editor scored it 4 because the AI section opens on the tool, not on her: "AI does a lot of my research and most of the typing in my builds." The first catch also makes the tool its subject: "When AI Docs Watch reported on Anthropic’s usage policy rewrite, it had written about the wrong part". What works is the Garde story, which every buyer remembered and which is the only proof under "Some tasks don’t need software, and I say so." The design critic and the copy editor would book once the AI section leads with her and one captured report shows the catch.

8. **Cut "LinkedIn Email"** under "A short introduction you can pass along" (design critic); the footer carries both.

## Disagreements

- **Cut Garde or keep it.** The CMO wanted it cut as a maintenance worry. The agency founder showed it is the only proof under "Some tasks don’t need software, and I say so." The CMO withdrew. Chair's call: keep it, trimmed.
- **Commercial terms on About.** The agency founder wanted volume, turnaround, overlap hours, white-label and slots in "Work with me". The Head of Content argued terms belong on /services, one idea in one place. The founder narrowed it to one sentence and a clearer link. Chair's call: the founder's final version.
- **Three artefacts or one.** The design critic wanted three captured artefacts. The copy editor showed two repeat proof shown elsewhere. The design critic conceded. Chair's call: the Docs Watch report only.
- **A stage strip for "How an article gets made".** The Head of Content wanted named stages; the design critic called a stage strip a rejected pattern. Chair's call: one prose sentence saying review is a separate pass.
