## Disagreements

- **Does the page have an outcome?** The CMO says no (no hours saved, no price). The Head of Content says the outcome of a monitor is a change caught in time, and the page has it: "We captured it on October 7 and caught the change on October 14, but the new policy itself doesn't take effect until November 12, 2026", four weeks of warning on a named channel. The debate did not move the CMO. Chair's call: the Head of Content is right about what a monitor proves, and the CMO is right that the page never says it that way. The four-week lead belongs in the first section, in those words.
- **Retitle for search.** The Head of Content ranked a search-targeted title above the usage gap; the founder argued the searcher for "a daily monitor of AI documentation changes" wants the monitor and leaves through "Open AI Docs Watch", so the page would compete with the tool's own site. Chair's call: the title now says what the tool is and who it is for, without chasing the tool's own query.
- **"anyone, it's public" undermines the offer.** The founder read it as "why pay for what I can read free"; the CMO showed the offer is a custom build and the public tool is the proof, not the product. The founder conceded. Chair's call: keep the tool public, replace the status with use.

## Devil's advocate

The agency founder argued that the page already proves use without a count: three catches across three months, a named role complaining about a gap, a CTO running it in production. It moved the founder's own ask from "a user count" to "the hours it replaced", and it moved nobody off change 1.
