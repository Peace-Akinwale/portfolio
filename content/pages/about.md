## Here’s my research process to create product-led content

My research includes:

- **SERP analysis** to understand search intent and content gaps
- **Social listening** on LinkedIn, Twitter, and industry podcasts (thanks to *ListenNotes*) for expert insights
- Comprehensive competitor analysis using SEMrush
- I use the product and take screenshots or GIFs to show how the software works for a specific use case.

### My research process in detail:

- I paste the keyword on SEMrush *keyword overview* to **find competing pages** for the keyword.

  I read these blogs to understand the **search intent they served**, the **gaps in the content** (whether in outline, structure, or context), and **how I can improve on what they did to rank**. SEMrush also suggests helpful LSI keywords to optimize the page.
- If there are gaps in client expectations & my research (on content idea or approach), I communicate it upfront so we can be on the same page. If there isn’t, I continue.
- I read low-performing pages, too. It’s where the gems are sometimes, and I take notes to improve my arguments or knowledge on the topic.
- I use **LinkedIn**, **YouTube**, **Twitter**, and **podcasts** to find expert insights when I don’t have an SME to speak to or a tight deadline. I paste a keyword (it doesn’t have to be my primary keyword) into the social platform’s search bar to find context for my topic, and I give credit to the expert when I copy-paste their words.
- For podcasts, I use [Listennotes](https://www.listennotes.com/?srsltid=AfmBOopSSfiXQmbENGMk9dCqurMT_ZijiMxI0382m4FvdDUPO2XG4OB5) to find pods related to my topic.

- I use my notes and existing research to build an outline.
- If I need to feature product use cases, I would use the product and jot down features relevant to the article I am writing. I’ll also save some screenshots.
- I keep my 20+ other tabs open so I can write and double-check if I misinterpreted a statement, idea, or framework.
- I dump my draft on Grammarly, fix the little grammar errors, and paste it back to GDocs. I don’t write & edit…helps me articulate my thoughts better.
- Here’s where the real work begins.
- I start tearing my piece down. I fix incoherent sentences and ideas, fact-check, add new stats, reshuffle ideas, add internal and external links, and add screenshots and images to give more context—you get it…
- Then, I edit again manually.
- I add alt texts to the images.
- And edit again. And again.
- Of course, I give it time to breathe so I can spot the gaps in my content.
