### What Google's raters look for, applied to every page

Google's human quality raters review pages using the Search Quality Rater Guidelines: does the page show first-hand experience, is it clear who's responsible for it, are its claims accurate, does it earn trust for its purpose? Agencies already apply this thinking by hand during content audits, page by page. Quality Rater runs the same questions across a whole site.


### Give the strategist a starting point for the audit

After a site is checked, the run screen groups high-priority pages and recurring findings. A strategist can start by investigating a problem that appears across several pages instead of opening every report in turn. These are suggested priorities for human review, not proof that the pages are poor.


### Show the passage behind each proposed fix

Each report links its finding to a passage and explains the proposed fix. The strategist can inspect that passage before deciding whether to accept the recommendation, reject it or return to it later. Recording a reason keeps the reviewer’s judgment alongside the model’s suggestion.


A reviewer can request a rewrite through the draft checker. A separate writing model prepares it, with checks intended to reject changes to quoted source material. This keeps rewriting tied to a human decision rather than automatically acting on every flagged issue.

### Tie model findings to numbered sentences

I split pages into numbered sentences so the decision model could identify a specific location instead of offering a vague criticism of the whole page. A second pass checks those selections before the finding is shown. That makes a recommendation easier for a strategist to examine, although it does not establish that the judgment is correct.

I directed the first build with AI coding agents over about two weeks in October 2026. It went live for the agency team on October 13.

### Still in progress

This is an independent tool built for the agency. Agreement with human reviewers has not yet been measured.
