# Decisions — peaceakinwale.com

## Goal
A portfolio site that lets a B2B SaaS head of content or founder decide, in a few minutes, to book a call with Peace, and that does not read as AI-generated.

## Constraints
- Stack: Next.js 16 App Router, Tailwind 4, hosted on Vercel. Blog posts, About and Portfolio body copy come from WordPress.com (`peaceakinwale.wordpress.com`, public REST API, env `WORDPRESS_SITE`) since commit `22ad8a8`. The folder `lib/hashnode/` and the `Hashnode*` type names are leftovers from before that switch; Hashnode is not used. Editing that copy happens in WordPress, not here.
- Contact email goes through Resend (`app/api/contact/route.ts`). Booking goes through Calendly.
- Work stays local on a branch until Peace reviews it. No push, no deploy without her say-so.
- Facts (client numbers, article counts, job titles, prices) come from Peace or a live source, never invented.

## 2026-10-29: MyLinks recommendations, where Jev fits (proposal, not built)

- **Why.** Peace, 29 Oct: MyLinks should work better, and Jev can do the link-recommendation part. Jev is TypeSafe's decision model, paid for through her OpenRouter account since 24 Oct (`typesafe/jev-1.14`, $0.042 per 1M input tokens, output free). It returns probabilities, never text.
- **How suggestions work today** (`lib/mylinks/ai.ts`, `app/api/mylinks/articles/[articleId]/suggest/route.ts`). The article is embedded once and `match_pages` returns the 80 crawled pages closest to the whole article, plus client URLs. One `gpt-4o` prompt then does everything at once: picks destinations, picks anchor phrases, guesses character offsets, scores itself and writes a justification. Articles over 1,500 words are split into chunks that each get the same 80-page inventory. Offsets are re-resolved by string search, case-insensitive on the second pass.
- **What is weak, read from the live data** (ManyRequests project, 371 pages, 21 landing pages).
  - The scores mean nothing. All 15 suggestions on "Agency Retainer Pricing" sit between 0.80 and 1.00, 12 of them "high". The relevance bar in the review UI is the model grading its own homework.
  - Blog bias. 14 of those 15 point at `/blog/`; the billing software page never appears under "fixed monthly fee" even though the prompt asks for commercial pages. The inventory is ranked against the whole article, so topical posts crowd out product pages.
  - Coverage is erratic. A 4,511-word article got 3 suggestions. A 3,128-word one got 15. The prompt begs for "15 or more", which is not a method.
  - Anchors land on single generic words ("SEO", "track time", "admin work"), and the case-insensitive fallback can pin an anchor to a different occurrence from the one the model meant.
  - Cost. `llm_usage_logs` shows $0.05 to $0.13 per article at gpt-4o rates, most of it the inventory repeated per chunk.
  - Nothing checks the output. The justification is written after the choice and can say anything.
- **Where Jev fits.** Jev scores pairs that code proposes. Code splits the draft into sentences (outside headings and existing links), embeds them with the existing `text-embedding-3-small`, and pairs each candidate destination with its closest sentences. Jev answers, per pair, one yes/no question: would a reader of this sentence be served by this page. The probability becomes the relevance score, a real calibrated number, and the cut-off is 0.7 (TypeSafe's suggested starting point). Code applies the rules the prompt used to beg for: one link per destination, one per sentence, commercial pages win ties. The anchor phrase is a Jev `choice` over spans of the chosen sentence, ordered by overlap with the page title so the model's known lean to the first option works for us. No text is written in this path, so gpt-4o is not called. The justification is built from facts (the score, the page title). gpt-4o stays as the fallback when the key is missing or Jev fails, with a message the user can see.
- **Facts verified from the docs today** (docs.typesafe.ai, openrouter.ai/docs/guides/community/jev). Endpoint `POST {JEV_BASE_URL}/systemone` with `Authorization: Bearer`, body `{model, state, questions}`; `noul` returns `noul` 0 to 1 with optional `criteria.true/false`; `choice` takes up to 255 options and returns `choice`, `confidence`, `probabilities`; response carries `usage.input_tokens` and, through OpenRouter, `usage.cost`. Default context is 64k per request and 32k per question; states over about 32,000 bytes switch to a slower, less accurate long-context mode. Input is billed once per request however many questions ride on it (13 questions in one call cost $0.000497 in TypeSafe's own test). Retry 429 and 529 with backoff, never 400, 401 or 422. Known weaknesses (1.13 page, no 1.14 page yet): first-option lean in `choice`, accuracy falls as the state grows with unrelated content, literal reading of the question.
- **Env names**, the same as the contentDB spec: `JEV_BASE_URL` (default `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1`), `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `JEV_MODEL` (pinned `typesafe/jev-1.14`). Peace creates and adds the key; no session handles its value.
- **No new dependency.** The TypeSafe SDK exists (`@typesafe-ai/sdk`) but the call is one `fetch` with a JSON body, so a 60-line client in `lib/mylinks/jev.ts` does it without adding a package.
- **Cost per article (estimate).** 80 destinations, one request each with 4 sentence questions at about 400 tokens: about 32,000 input tokens, $0.0013. Sentence embeddings about $0.0001. Anchor choices for 15 to 25 accepted pairs, under $0.0005. About $0.002 per article against $0.05 to $0.13 today. Latency about 5 seconds at 6 concurrent requests, going by OpenRouter's measured 171 ms median for jev-1.13.
- **Status.** Proposal written for Peace's yes. Nothing built. Before-snapshot of the current suggestions for the two ManyRequests articles saved in the side-session notes folder.
